The prison system Florida called progress

J. C. Powell's The American Siberia is a standalone investigation of convict leasing, prison labor, camp administration, and the violence hidden inside institutional language.

Powell's account, on its own terms

Powell's 1891 book describes a Florida convict camp from the position of a camp captain. He writes about leased prisoners, railroad construction, guards, escape, food, punishment, and the unstable boundary between public custody and private labor.

This page does not connect Powell's account to Fortune Ferguson or the Newberry Six. The records concern different inquiries. Powell belongs to the history of Florida's prison and convict-labor system; Fortune and Newberry belong together on a separate page because both family histories are rooted in the Newberry region.

A prison-system history can stand on its own without becoming an explanation for every later Florida case.

Reading an insider source

Powell's proximity to authority is part of the evidence. His narrative can reveal how coercion was organized while also normalizing the institution that produced it. Read for what is named, what is omitted, and how incarcerated people are converted into labor, risk, or management problems.

Research questions

  • How did convict leasing connect public punishment to private profit?
  • How did guards, transportation, food, discipline, and escape shape daily life?
  • Which voices are absent from Powell's account?
  • What prison registers, court files, medical records, and testimony can test his narrative?
